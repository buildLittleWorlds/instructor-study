1 · Today’s course practice

Debug the relationship between idea, interaction, and claim

A technical check can tell us whether the Life engine follows its rule. It cannot decide whether the interaction makes the intended question visible—or whether the explanation claims more than the interaction shows.

1

Run one bounded check

Name what you expect, perform one repeatable action, and record what actually happens.

2

Locate the layer

The problem may be in code, instructions, visual emphasis, the central idea, or the claim—not simply “the app.”

3

Change one thing and rerun

Keep the same check when possible. A changed result becomes evidence about the revision.

3 · Paper layer

What the first version lets me say—and not say

Question

How can a pattern remain one thing when the parts that physically instantiate it change?

Observation

From generation 0 to generation 1, three live positions remain occupied, two become empty, and two previously empty positions become alive.

Interpretation

Fixed coordinates do not provide a sufficient boundary for the moving glider. Pattern and continuous causal history become stronger candidates.

Limitation

The simulation verifies the changing cells. It does not prove a philosophical definition of identity, nor show that every kind of thing persists like a glider.

Current claim · Version 1

A thing can sometimes persist through a change of parts, but calling it “the same thing” requires a traceable continuity—such as a recurring pattern under a stable rule—not a name alone.

Source and method note. The glider is a five-cell spaceship in Conway’s Game of Life. After four generations it returns to its original orientation displaced one cell diagonally. The page computes B3/S23 directly and checks generations 0–4 against an independent fixture. For a reference description, see the LifeWiki glider entry. The prose and identity interpretation are authored for this course.
4 · Development record

The revision is part of the artifact

This ledger separates what happened from what I decided to do about it. Later versions will carry it forward rather than pretending the finished app appeared all at once.

Kept
The Game of Life remains the starting medium, and the glider remains a concrete object whose cells can be inspected exactly.
Tried
I changed the central action from repairing a deliberately broken program to drawing a boundary around what the reader thinks persists.
Encountered
The engine check passes, yet marking the original five coordinates fails to follow two newly born cells. Correct computation does not settle the identity question.
Changed
The page now places the technical check beside a conceptual check: what did the interaction actually let the reader notice?
Rejected
I did not add image recognition, embeddings, or an AI judgment. Those would arrive before the human boundary-making action was understandable.
Current claim
Continuity can outlast particular parts, but it must be shown through a pattern, rule, or history rather than merely asserted.
